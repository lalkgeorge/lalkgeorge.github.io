The B2B SaaS tracking checklist: GA4, GTM & HubSpot

UTM rules, the events that matter, getting source data into the CRM, and sending better conversions back to ad platforms.

LG
Lal K GeorgeOct 20, 2026 · 7 min read

Every marketing decision you make is only as good as the data behind it. And in most B2B SaaS companies I've seen, that data has holes: missing UTMs, forms that don't fire events, demo bookings tracked twice, and a CRM that has no idea which ad brought a lead in.

Here's the tracking checklist I use with GA4, Google Tag Manager and HubSpot. It isn't glamorous, but it's the foundation everything else sits on.

1. Foundations

  • One GTM container on every page, including the app signup and booking pages, not just the marketing site.
  • GA4 configured through GTM, not hard-coded, so changes don't need developers.
  • Internal traffic filtered (office IPs, your team) and referral exclusions set for payment and booking tools, so sessions don't break in the middle of a conversion.
  • Cross-domain tracking if your site and app live on different domains.
  • Consent mode set up properly for the regions you sell into.

2. A UTM convention everyone follows

Inconsistent UTMs are the most common reason channel reports don't add up. Decide once, write it down, and use a shared builder sheet.

utm_source   = platform   (meta, google, linkedin, x, newsletter)
utm_medium   = type       (paid_social, cpc, email, organic_social)
utm_campaign = name       (2026q4_webinar_retarget)
utm_content  = ad/variant (video_hook_a)

Always lowercase. No spaces. Never tag internal links.

3. Track the events that matter

Page views are not a funnel. Track the actions that show intent, and mark the important ones as key events (conversions) in GA4:

EventWhy it matters
generate_leadAny form fill. Include the form name as a parameter.
demo_bookedYour most important signal. Fire it on actual booking confirmation, not the button click.
sign_upTrial or free-plan signups.
pricing_viewStrong intent signal, great for retargeting and lead scoring.
content_downloadTop-of-funnel engagement.
Common mistake

Firing the conversion on button click instead of on success. You end up counting failed submissions and people who closed the calendar without booking.

4. Get the source into the CRM

GA4 tells you about sessions. Your CRM tells you about revenue. The magic happens when they're connected:

  • Capture UTMs and click IDs (gclid, fbclid, li_fat_id) in hidden form fields and store them on the HubSpot contact.
  • Keep original source (first touch) and latest source separate.
  • Make sure HubSpot's tracking code and forms run on every page with a form.

5. Send conversions back to the ad platforms

Ad algorithms optimize for whatever you tell them is a conversion. If that's a form fill, they'll find cheap form fills. Send them better signals:

  • Meta Conversions API alongside the pixel, with deduplication.
  • Google Ads enhanced conversions plus offline conversion imports for SQLs and opportunities.
  • LinkedIn Insight Tag and conversions API for key events.

6. QA before you trust it

  • Test every form and booking flow in GTM preview and GA4 DebugView.
  • Check that one real conversion shows up once, not twice, in GA4, the ad platform and HubSpot.
  • Re-test after every site release. Tracking breaks silently.

The one-page version

  • GTM everywhere, GA4 through GTM, internal traffic filtered
  • Written UTM convention, lowercase, shared builder
  • Key events fire on success, not on click
  • UTMs and click IDs stored in the CRM, first and latest touch
  • Server-side or offline conversions sent back to Meta, Google and LinkedIn
  • QA after every release

Clean tracking won't make your campaigns better by itself. It will stop you from confidently scaling the wrong ones. Questions? Find me on X or LinkedIn.
