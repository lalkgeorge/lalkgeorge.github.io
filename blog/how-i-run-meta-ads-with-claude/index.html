How I run Meta Ads with Claude: my actual workflow

Claude connected straight to the ad account turns hours of spreadsheet audits into a few questions. Here's the setup, the weekly prompts, and the rules I don't break.

For years, my Monday morning looked the same: export Meta Ads data, paste it into a sheet, build pivots, squint at frequency columns, and try to remember which ad set I changed last Tuesday. By the time I had answers, half the morning was gone.

Today a large part of that work runs through Claude connected directly to the ad account. I ask questions in plain English, Claude pulls the numbers, and I spend my time on decisions instead of spreadsheets. This post is the exact workflow.

Why I put Claude next to Meta Ads

Meta's own interface is great at showing you data and terrible at telling you what matters. The questions I actually care about cut across campaigns, date ranges and breakdowns:

  • Which ads are spending without producing a single conversion?
  • Where is frequency climbing while CTR drops (creative fatigue)?
  • Are two ad sets quietly bidding against each other for the same audience?
  • Which creative angles (not just individual ads) are winning?

Each of those used to be a mini analysis project. With an AI assistant that can read the account, each one becomes a single question.

The setup

The core idea is simple: give Claude read access to Meta Ads data through an integration (a connector or MCP server for the Meta Marketing API), so it works with live numbers instead of whatever I remember to paste in.

  1. Connect the ad account through an integration that supports Meta Ads. Start with read-only access.
  2. Give Claude context once: the business, the funnel, what counts as a conversion, target CPL/CPA, and naming conventions. I keep this in a short brief Claude can refer back to.
  3. Connect the CRM side (for me, HubSpot) so "conversions" can be checked against leads that actually became pipeline, not just form fills.
Tip

The brief matters more than the prompts. "Our target CPL is X, anything over 1.5× for 7 days is a problem, and we care about SQLs, not raw leads" turns generic analysis into analysis of your account.

My weekly audit, step by step

Every week I run roughly the same sequence. These are the kinds of prompts I use:

1. Wasted spend

List every ad that spent more than [amount] in the last 14 days
with zero conversions. Group by campaign and sort by spend.

2. Creative fatigue

Find ads where frequency has risen above 3 over the last 2 weeks
while CTR has dropped more than 20% from their first week.

3. Audience overlap and learning phase

Which ad sets are stuck in the learning phase, and why? Are any
ad sets targeting overlapping audiences in the same campaign?

4. Budget pacing

Which campaigns consistently underspend their daily budget,
and what's the most likely reason?

5. Placement and funnel mix

Break down spend and CPL by placement (Feed, Reels, Stories,
Audience Network) and by cold vs. retargeting audiences.

Claude returns a short list of findings and suggested actions. I then check the important ones in Ads Manager myself before anything changes.

Creative and copy

The second big use is creative. Once Claude knows which ads and angles are working, it's very good at:

  • Spotting patterns across winners: pain-led vs. outcome-led hooks, proof vs. curiosity, video vs. static.
  • Writing new variations of winning angles, within Meta's character limits.
  • Proposing a simple test plan: what to test next, how to split budget, and when to call a winner.

I still decide what goes live. AI is fast at producing options; judgment about the brand and the buyer stays with me.

The rules I don't break

  • Read first, write later. I start every new setup read-only. Changes to budgets or status are made deliberately, by a human.
  • New campaigns start paused. Nothing spends money without a final human check.
  • Verify the surprising stuff. If a finding looks too good or too bad, confirm it in Ads Manager. Attribution settings and date ranges can change the story.
  • Close the loop in the CRM. A cheap lead that never becomes an opportunity isn't cheap. Judge Meta on pipeline, not form fills.
  • No sensitive data in prompts. Customer lists and personal data stay out of chat.

How to start this week

  1. Connect Claude to your Meta Ads account read-only.
  2. Write a five-line brief: business, conversion event, target CPL/CPA, what "bad" looks like.
  3. Run just the wasted spend and creative fatigue prompts above.
  4. Act on the top 3 findings, and compare next week.
The goal isn't to let AI run your ads. It's to stop spending your best hours finding problems, so you can spend them fixing them.
