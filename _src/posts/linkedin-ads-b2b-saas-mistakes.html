LinkedIn Ads has the best B2B targeting of any platform, and some of the highest costs per click. That combination is unforgiving: small setup mistakes burn budget fast. These are the seven I see most often in B2B SaaS accounts.

1. Targeting that's too narrow (or too broad)

Stacking job title + seniority + company size + industry + skills can shrink an audience to a few thousand people, and delivery stalls or costs spike. On the other side, broad job functions without seniority filters waste spend on people who will never buy. For most campaigns, aim for an audience in the tens of thousands, and use job function + seniority or company lists (ABM) rather than long title lists.

2. Leaving Audience Expansion and LinkedIn Audience Network on

Both are often enabled by default. Audience Expansion loosens your targeting; the Audience Network places ads outside LinkedIn. Turn both off at the start, measure, and only test them deliberately later.

3. Sending cold audiences straight to "Book a demo"

Someone who has never heard of you is unlikely to book a call from one ad. Build a simple sequence instead:

  1. Cold: a useful insight, short video or document ad.
  2. Warm: retarget people who engaged with proof: case studies, customer results.
  3. Hot: demo or trial offer to visitors of pricing and product pages.

4. Ignoring Lead Gen Form quality

Lead Gen Forms convert well because LinkedIn pre-fills them, which also means low-intent leads come in easily. Add one qualifying question, sync the forms to your CRM immediately, and follow up the same day. Judge them on SQL rate, not cost per lead.

5. Too few creatives per campaign

With small audiences, frequency climbs fast and performance drops. Run 3 to 5 creatives per campaign and refresh regularly. Test formats too: document ads and short videos often outperform single images for B2B.

6. Optimizing for clicks instead of outcomes

If the campaign objective and conversion tracking aren't set up around the action you actually want, LinkedIn will find people who like to click. Install the Insight Tag, set up conversions for real actions, and connect CRM outcomes so you can report cost per SQL.

7. Judging LinkedIn by the same rules as Meta or Google

LinkedIn clicks are expensive, so a channel-level CPL comparison almost always makes it look bad. But the leads often come from exactly the accounts sales wants. Compare channels on cost per opportunity and pipeline value, and look at influenced pipeline from your target account list.

Quick audit

Open your account and check: audience size, Audience Expansion off, LAN off, at least 3 creatives per campaign, Insight Tag firing, lead forms synced to the CRM. That 10-minute check catches most of the waste.
